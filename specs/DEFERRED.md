## Superseded decisions

- **Rigid part-type slots** (fixed "engine slot," "aero slot," etc.) were
	rejected because they made off-type items illegal. The later vehicle-topology
	decision supersedes the generic-slot conclusion without reviving that hard
	restriction: every item remains legal in every Power, Chassis, or Flex slot,
	and placement changes visible item-authored behavior. See
	`specs/vehicle-topology.md`.

## Considered and rejected (not deferred — decided against)

For completeness, not everything raised in design conversations gets built later. These were considered and explicitly rejected, with reasoning, so nobody re-litigates them from scratch:

- **Spatial/zone-based chassis diagram** (Backpack Battles-style physical packing) — even its own original proposer agreed this game's fun is the manager decision, not the geometry; the visual/UI cost isn't justified here.
- **Slot count varying by team identity** (e.g., a Performance team simply gets more total slots) — creates a strictly dominant strategy when every item lives in one undifferentiated pool (more slots is always better, unlike Hearthstone Battlegrounds' hero powers, which differ in kind, not just in scalar advantage). Identity belongs in the items and their synergies, not in container size.
- **Named capacity pools tied to identity** (e.g., "Chassis" vs. "Team" slots) — explored seriously, three of four design personas endorsed it, but ultimately set aside in favor of the simpler flat-slots-plus-item-synergy approach once the slot-count-variance idea was ruled out for the same underlying reason (it re-introduces a category system the owner already rejected once).
- **Homologation lock-in** (can't swap a build between prepare and contest once "locked") — was proposed as a companion to a continuous-budget system; the motorsport-engineer persona who proposed it agreed it doesn't solve a real problem once the constraint is a discrete slot count instead.

**Convention**: add a row whenever a spec/clarify/plan session defers something explicitly (as opposed to deciding against it). Note which feature deferred it, why, and — if one exists — which constitutional principle guarantees it gets picked back up. When a later feature ships and resolves an entry, strike through its "Deferred in" cell, prefix it with **RESOLVED by** or **PARTIALLY RESOLVED by** and the resolving feature, and update the "Where it's protected" cell to say what actually shipped — don't delete the row, so the history of what was deferred and for how long stays visible.

# Initial Release Roster

*Authoritative character direction for the initial release. Mechanical details
remain subject to feature specifications, but the four entrants, their item
ecosystems, and the identity model below are committed product direction.*

## Roster principle

A playable character defines an **approach to racing**, not a prescribed build
strategy. Each entrant brings a thematic ecosystem of items drawn from their
profession, community, and experience before organized auto racing existed.
That ecosystem must support several viable strategies and must combine
meaningfully with items originating from other entrants.

Character choice determines:

- the entrant's identity, personality, machine silhouette, and presentation;
- which origin-tagged items the draft is weighted toward;
- the named vehicle's distribution of Power, Chassis, and Flex slots;
- the thematic vocabulary through which common strategies are expressed;
- character-specific dialogue and narrative framing.

Character choice does not determine:

- baseline pace, total active item capacity, storage capacity, or contest rules;
- one mandatory win condition or exclusive mechanical archetype;
- which strategies the player is allowed to pursue;
- a closed item pool that prevents cross-character builds.

## Item identity model

Future item content must distinguish **origin**, **installation category**, and
**mechanical synergy**.

- **Origin** identifies the entrant ecosystem that normally offers the item:
  `coachworks`, `velodrome`, `fieldworks`, or `backroads`.
- **Installation category** identifies whether the item is Power or Chassis and
  controls its relationship to the vehicle slot.
- **Synergy tags** describe what the item interacts with, such as `wheel`,
  `momentum`, `tool`, `heat`, `cargo`, `pressure`, or `wager`.
- Draft weighting reads origin. Build effects should primarily read synergy
  tags, item state, and observable race events.
- Items from different origins may share synergy tags. This is intentional and
  is the foundation for creative cross-character builds.
- Occasional off-origin offers are required. An entrant's ecosystem is a draft
  bias, not an ownership restriction.

Every ecosystem must contain meaningful Power and Chassis items. Installation
category must not become a second origin label. See `specs/vehicle-topology.md`
for Fitted, Flexible, and Improvised behavior.

An item may express familiar strategies such as pace, scaling, control,
economy, reliability, or risk, but no origin owns any one of those strategies.

## Evelyn Mercer - Coachworks

**Role**: Master coachwright and accomplished carriage racer

**Signature machine**: *The Highwheel*, a four-wheel motor carriage built with
fine hardwood, sprung leather, brass fittings, and controls adapted from reins.

**Approach to racing**: Motor racing may be new, but craftsmanship, road sense,
and competitive driving are not. Evelyn treats the championship as both a race
and the first true public trial of the coachbuilder's art.

**Character**: Exacting, composed, and dryly competitive. Evelyn welcomes the
machine age because she intends to shape it, but she is privately aware that the
skills and workshops she represents may be made obsolete by its success. She
respects proven work, dislikes waste, and judges every wild invention first by
the quality of its joinery.

**Ecosystem vocabulary**:

- coachbuilding, wheels, suspension, steering, harness, leather, wood, and brass;
- roadcraft, durability, comfort, balance, passengers, and fine materials;
- workshops, commissions, appraisal, refurbishment, trade-ins, and clientele.

**Supported strategy directions**:

- dependable recurring effects and conventional direct performance;
- durable or defensive builds that protect accumulated advantages;
- wheel, material, matching-set, and held-item-count synergies;
- momentum, weight, and road-condition interactions;
- value builds based on improving, appraising, refurbishing, or reselling items;
- commission builds that reward demonstrating particular combinations or outcomes.

**Economic axis**: **Craftsmanship and resale.** Coachworks items can gain,
preserve, or realize value through use, refinement, matching sets, trade-ins,
and patron commissions. This replaces delivery or mail as the ecosystem's
economic identity. It must support both patient investment and active item
cycling rather than rewarding only one optimal holding pattern.

**Example item directions**: Hickory Spokes, Racing Reins, Masterwork Leaf
Springs, Morocco Leather Seat, Brass Wheel Hub, Client's Commission, Appraiser's
Loupe, and Refurbished Axle.

## Lucien Soto - Velodrome

**Role**: Champion bicycle racer and celebrated velodrome sprinter

**Signature machine**: *The Needle*, a narrow motor cycle built around a light
tubular frame, exposed chain drive, and wheels that seem far too slender for its
engine.

**Approach to racing**: The machine is an extension of the competitor. Lucien
understands rhythm, drafting, positioning, endurance, and the precise instant a
race can be won.

**Character**: Magnetic in public and intensely analytical in competition.
Lucien notices tiny changes in cadence and position, then describes them with
the confidence of settled fact. He takes concern about his fragile-looking
machine as an insult, even while depending on mechanics more than his athletic
philosophy admits.

**Ecosystem vocabulary**:

- bicycles, chains, sprockets, tyres, tubular frames, bells, and racing silks;
- cadence, momentum, drafting, balance, stamina, recovery, and final sprints;
- spectators, sporting clubs, prize purses, records, and athletic training.

**Supported strategy directions**:

- frequent triggers, cooldown coordination, and repeated-effect builds;
- momentum or cadence that rewards maintaining a sequence;
- drafting and position-sensitive racecraft;
- lightweight, low-count, or precisely curated builds;
- stamina and late-race scaling;
- explosive sprint finishes and risk-reward pacing;
- crowd, record, and prize-purse value builds.

**Economic axis**: **Prizes and acclaim.** Velodrome items can convert records,
streaks, close racing, crowd response, or declared athletic challenges into
value. The ecosystem may pursue steady club support or gamble on difficult
headline performances.

**Example item directions**: Champion's Sprocket, Pacemaker's Bell, Silk Racing
Jersey, Wooden Training Rollers, Spare Inner Tube, Spectator's Pennant,
Final-Sprint Tonic, and Tandem Crank.

## Inez Rook - Fieldworks

**Role**: Expedition engineer and experimental vehicle designer

**Signature machine**: *The Lark*, a modular experimental machine whose current
three-wheel layout, rear propeller, instrument array, and structural test mounts
let Inez evaluate technologies borrowed from aircraft, industrial machinery,
and expedition transport in one racing platform.

**Approach to racing**: No existing vehicle category gets to decide what belongs
in a racing machine. Inez transfers serious technology across disciplines and
uses competition to discover combinations that convention would never test.

**Character**: Animated, persuasive, and delighted by an unanswered engineering
question. Inez is technically confident and audacious without being careless:
every experiment has a hypothesis, instrumentation, and a reason to exist. She
is slower to trust people than machines, but the championship is her first
chance to apply her expedition experience to joyful, open-ended invention and
personal ambition rather than necessity.

Her past centers on expeditions, remote works, recovery, and practical field
engineering. Any military history remains background texture rather than the
source of jokes, weapons, or a combat-centered identity.

**Ecosystem vocabulary**:

- aircraft engines, propellers, streamlined canvas, and experimental airflow;
- pressure-fed fuel systems, cooling jackets, pumps, gauges, and control valves;
- multi-axle steering, articulated wheel bogies, structural tubing, and test instrumentation;
- technology transfer, modular prototypes, controlled limits, and cross-disciplinary integration.

**Supported strategy directions**:

- aircraft power, propeller, airflow, and lightweight combinations;
- three-, four-, or six-wheel tracking and articulated running-gear systems;
- pressure, heat, cooling, fuel, and instrumented controlled-limit builds;
- contextual prototypes that excel in particular track conditions while making visible tradeoffs elsewhere;
- multi-stat and multi-tag assemblies that connect Power and Chassis systems;
- stacking experimental improvements whose performance envelope grows under observation.

**Economic axis**: **Prototype development and technology transfer.** Fieldworks
extracts value from proving that equipment developed for another discipline has
a competitive application. Experimental instruments, test results, licensing,
and cross-industry sponsorship may reward players for assembling and validating
unconventional systems. This axis celebrates invention; it must not imply that
Inez competes with inferior materials or only succeeds by using discarded parts.

**Example item directions**: Aircraft Engine Cradle, Variable-Pitch Propeller,
Streamlined Balloon Fabric, Pressure-Fed Carburetor, Six-Wheel Tracking Bogie,
Articulated Steering Linkage, Calibrated Pressure Manifold, and Instrumented
Cooling Jacket.

Flight remains an item effect, visual flourish, or build expression unless a
later feature explicitly designs a shared movement rule. *The Lark* does not
bypass laps or use a private simulation model.

## Nell Voss - Backroads

**Role**: Customs runner, rulebook engineer, and veteran of races that were
never officially announced

**Signature machine**: *The Hush*, a low, stripped roadster with shuttered
lamps, concealed compartments, interchangeable plates, and more fuel capacity
than its paperwork admits.

**Approach to racing**: Officials enforce written rules, not intentions, and
rivals defend against expected moves, not every possible one. Nell treats the
rulebook and accepted racing etiquette as assumptions waiting to be tested. She
builds to the exact written limit, finds the unguarded interpretation or line,
and commits before it closes.

**Character**: Watchful, self-possessed, and unexpectedly generous to people
who keep their word. Nell dislikes romantic stories about outlaws and treats
illicit work as a trade requiring preparation, nerve, and reliable partners.
She is controlled, opportunistic, and more aggressive than she first appears:
if an overconfident rival leaves a braking zone or racing line undefended, she
will commit before anyone else recognizes the opening. Her deeper goal is to
win openly enough that nobody can dismiss her skill as a trick of the night roads.

Nell's aggression has a firm boundary. She may exploit measurement procedures,
stretch technical definitions, conceal capacity, pressure a rival into a
mistake, brake exceptionally late, or use an unfashionable line. She does not
tamper with another machine, deploy hazards, or deliberately cause a collision.

Backroads draws on smugglers, customs runners, bookmakers, informal road races,
and illicit transport broadly. It is not tied specifically to American
Prohibition or one real culture's criminal history.

**Ecosystem vocabulary**:

- contraband, hidden compartments, false manifests, disguises, and interchangeable plates;
- lookouts, informants, bribes, bookmakers, fences, wagers, and black markets;
- shuttered lamps, shortcuts, evasion, decoys, distractions, and road hazards;
- suspicion, exposure, risk, escape, and favors owed.

**Supported strategy directions**:

- rulebook engineering through exact counts, exact compositions, declared values, and technical loopholes;
- late-braking attacks and aggressive corner entry with explicit performance tradeoffs;
- information builds that identify narrow track or performance opportunities;
- exposure builds that exchange general safety margin for exceptional pace in a precise situation;
- wagers and conditional high-roll effects;
- sudden getaway acceleration and decisive high-speed escape.

**Economic axis**: **Contraband and exposure.** Backroads can acquire illicit
goods that function as items, appreciate while carried, or sell through fences.
Powerful opportunities may also increase exposure to officials or other risks;
items can profit from, conceal, redirect, or clear that exposure. Early
implementations should express these concepts through visible items and effects,
not a character-only permanent meter.

**Example item directions**: Declared Fuel Measure, Removable Inspection
Ballast, Auxiliary Starting Tank, Adjustable Bodywork Stay, Stamped Compliance
Plate, Lookout's Timing Board, Late-Braking Equalizer, and Unmarked Route Book.

## Shared strategic coverage

Every ecosystem must be capable of supporting multiple broad strategic
families in its own language. This table is a content target, not a requirement
that every family receive identical item counts.

| Strategy family | Coachworks | Velodrome | Fieldworks | Backroads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pace | gearing and roadcraft | sprinting and cadence | overclocking and airflow | escape bursts and shortcuts |
| Scaling | craftsmanship and matching sets | momentum and stamina | assembly and heat | exposure and carried contraband |
| Control | stability and positioning | drafting and tempo | smoke, pressure, and disruption | distractions and sabotage |
| Economy | appraisal and commissions | prizes and acclaim | prototype development and technology transfer | contraband and wagers |
| Reliability | durable construction | recovery and rhythm | instrumentation and engineered redundancy | evasion and insurance |
| Risk | weight and costly luxury | exhaustion and sprint timing | controlled limits and experimental tradeoffs | exposure and conditional bets |

## Content guardrails

- Each entrant must launch with enough item variety to support at least three
  meaningfully different build directions before off-origin items are counted.
- No entrant may be summarized mechanically as only "the speed character,"
  "the cooldown character," "the scaling character," or "the cheating character."
- Economy is a strategic direction available within every ecosystem, expressed
  through different fiction and interactions rather than four isolated currencies.
- Cross-origin combinations must be deliberately authored, not treated as
  accidental edge cases. Shared synergy tags should create discoverable bridges.
- A character-specific item may be unusually compatible with its home ecosystem
  but must remain understandable and functional when offered to another entrant.
- Character flavor must never hide effect values, trigger conditions, prices,
  or race consequences.
